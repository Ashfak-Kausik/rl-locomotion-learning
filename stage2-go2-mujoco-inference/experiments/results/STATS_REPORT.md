# 30-Seed Statistical Re-run — Report

Generated by `compute_stats.py` from `exp1_velocity_sweep_30seed.csv`,
`exp2_gait_robustness_30seed.csv`, and `exp3_terrain_30seed.csv` (seeds
0-29, parallel harness, `harness.run_trials_parallel`). The original
5-seed files (`exp1_velocity_sweep.csv`, `exp2_gait_robustness.csv`,
`exp3_terrain.csv`) are untouched. Full numbers are in the four
`stats_*.csv` files in this directory; this document is the narrative
summary, answering the five questions asked directly and bluntly.

---

## 1. Do the 30-seed means agree with the 5-seed means?

**Yes — nothing moved materially.** Every exp1 and exp2 mean at n=30 is
within ±0.003 of its n=5 (sample-std, ddof=1) counterpart; most agree to
the 3rd decimal. What changed is precision, not the estimate: standard
errors and CI widths shrank roughly √6× as expected going from 5 to 30
trials.

Two genuine, if minor, refinements surfaced only because n grew:

- **Bound's height_std is not exactly zero-variance.** At 5 seeds, all
  five values were `0.0021` (reported std 0.000, and Task 2 confirmed
  this was a real, not fabricated, zero). At 30 seeds, the true spread is
  visible: 30 values split between `0.0021` and `0.0022` (sample SD
  ≈3.5×10⁻⁵). Trot's and pace's height_std, by contrast, are **still
  exactly constant across all 30 seeds** (`0.0060` and `0.0109`
  respectively, sample SD ~10⁻¹⁸, i.e. floating-point noise, not real
  variance). So the "posture is a converged, near-deterministic quantity"
  finding holds for trot/pace but was only *approximately* true for bound
  — a nuance invisible at n=5.
- **The 2 cm stair condition is far more variable than 5 seeds could show.**
  At n=5: dist 4.02±0.09, vx 0.034±0.013. At n=30: dist 4.09±0.31, vx
  0.062±0.050 — roughly 3-4× the spread. The condition is still classified
  traversable (mean distance 4.09 m > the 3.5 m threshold), but the 5-seed
  sample understated how marginal/fragile this specific condition is.

## 2. Does the velocity saturation near 0.55 m/s hold?

**Yes.** At n=30, cmd=1.50 m/s achieved velocity is 0.5576 m/s (95% CI
0.5533–0.5619), essentially unchanged from the corrected 5-seed value
(0.558) and from cmd=1.00's 0.5043 m/s (CI 0.5041–0.5044) — the CIs at
1.00 and 1.50 do not overlap, confirming the plateau is a real,
statistically resolved effect, not sampling noise mimicking saturation.

## 3. Is pace still significantly better than trot on tracking error at n=30?

**Yes, decisively.** Welch's t-test, tracking error, trot vs. pace:
**t=72.62, p=1.43×10⁻⁵⁸** (Holm-corrected across the 9-comparison family:
p=7.14×10⁻⁵⁸), **Cohen's d = 18.75**.

Read the effect size carefully: d=18.75 is not a typo, but it should not
be read on the usual small/medium/large (0.2/0.5/0.8) scale — those
thresholds assume noisy real-world measurements. Here, within-condition
variance is tiny by construction (a nearly-deterministic policy re-run
with small, bounded initial-condition perturbations — see AUDIT.md Part
C.1), so 30 trot trials and 30 pace trials occupy essentially
non-overlapping ranges (trot: 0.273±0.005; pace: 0.176±0.005). The
headline claim survives at full strength; if anything, n=30 makes the
separation look more extreme than the n=5 data already suggested, purely
because tighter CIs make the non-overlap unambiguous rather than
suggestive.

The same pattern holds for lateral drift (trot vs. pace: t=21.90,
p=9.74×10⁻²⁴, d=5.65) and for all three pairwise gait comparisons on both
metrics (see `stats_exp2_pairwise.csv`) — every comparison in the family
is significant after Holm correction.

**One comparison is reported but not meaningful: height_std, trot vs.
pace.** Both groups are constant to within floating-point noise (sample
SD ~10⁻¹⁸ each), so the pooled-variance denominator in Cohen's d and the
t-statistic is itself numerical noise — the script reports a nonsensical
t≈−6.8×10¹⁵. Do not cite this number. The honest statement is: trot's
height_std (0.0060) and pace's (0.0109) are two different constants, not
two distributions with a computable effect size, because there is no
within-group variance to compute one from.

## 4. Do the traversal limits (10°, 2 cm) hold at 30 seeds, and does 15° still fail?

**Yes to both, with one quantitatively important correction at 15°.**

| Condition | n=5 survival | n=30 survival (95% Wilson CI) | Traversable (n=30)? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10° slope | 5/5 (100%) | 30/30 = 100% (CI 88.6–100%) | **YES** — unchanged |
| 15° slope | 1/5 (20%) | 11/30 = **36.7%** (CI 21.9–54.5%) | **no** — unchanged (still <80%) |
| 20°/25° slope | 0/5 | 0/30 | no — unchanged |
| 2 cm stairs | 5/5 (100%) | 30/30 = 100% (CI 88.6–100%) | **YES** — unchanged |
| 5/8/12/16 cm stairs | 5/5 survival, stall | 30/30 survival, stall (dist ≈0.47 m, well under 3.5 m threshold) | no — unchanged |

**The 15° qualitative conclusion (not traversable, below the 80%
survival bar) does not change.** But the 5-seed estimate of 20% survival
undersold the true rate by roughly half — 36.7% is nearly double. With
only 5 Bernoulli draws, a true survival probability anywhere from ~7% to
~65% would plausibly produce a 1/5 observation; 20% was always a rough
point estimate with a wide implicit uncertainty, and 30 seeds resolves
that this policy actually clears 15° more often than not-quite-half the
time, not one time in five. This is exactly the kind of correction more
seeds are for: it does not overturn "10° is the traversable limit," but
it would be wrong to cite "20% survival at 15°" as a precise number
going forward — cite 36.7% (CI 21.9–54.5%).

## 5. Did any conclusion in the paper weaken or reverse?

**No conclusion reverses. One number (15° slope survival rate) was
imprecise at n=5 and is now corrected; two nuances invisible at n=5
became visible at n=30 (bound's height_std is not exactly zero-variance;
the 2 cm stair condition is more variable than it looked).** Specifically:

- Velocity saturates near 0.55 m/s: **holds** (Q2).
- Posture stability decoupled from velocity tracking: **holds** for
  trot/pace exactly, **holds approximately** for bound (Q1).
- Non-monotonic lateral drift with a minimum near cmd=1.0 m/s: **holds**
  — n=30 minimum is 0.0192 m/s (CI 0.0161–0.0223), still clearly below
  its neighbors (0.75: 0.135; 1.5: 0.347).
- Pace beats trot on tracking error and drift: **holds, more strongly
  resolved** (Q3).
- Gait tradeoff structure (pace = best tracking/drift, bound = most rigid
  posture but worst tracking): **holds**.
- 10° slope / 2 cm step traversal limits: **hold** (Q4), though the 2 cm
  condition is now known to be more variable than the 5-seed data showed.
- 15° slope fails the traversability criterion: **holds**, but the
  underlying survival-rate number the paper would cite should be updated
  from ~20% to ~37% (Q4).

None of this weakens the paper's headline claims. The corrections are
exactly the kind a reviewer would expect from an n=5→n=30 upgrade:
tighter, more defensible numbers, one meaningfully corrected probability
estimate, and two subtle findings that a 5-seed study could not have
detected either way.
